# Research chronology

The program began by treating text representation as an experimental variable, then narrowed through controlled coordinate/operation studies, normalization geometry, proof-first pruning, and task-aligned readout questions.

## Milestone arc

### 001–004: representation and mechanism isolation

The early notebooks ask whether alternative representations can preserve useful signal and then separate coordinate choice from the operations that consume those coordinates. Controlled ablations narrow a promising result toward a specific phase-aware mechanism.

### 005–008: the hypersphere result and its correction

A dramatic frozen endpoint motivates a normalization/hypersphere interpretation. Later trajectory, regularization, stopping, source-unit, Jacobian, and intervention work weakens the simple story. Formal derivation identifies exactly what normalization guarantees and what it does not.

### 009–011: proof-first research design

The theorem-ledger method, dynamic quotient framing, and geometry-from-invariance work shift the process: settle mathematical questions before launching training when a theorem or counterexample can prune the search space.

### 012–013: task-aligned natural-source evidence

The program moves from aggregate rank/loss toward distinctions aligned with the actual prediction task. Probe/selectivity/MDL work supports a bounded conclusion: useful information can be accessible in a state while the native consumer underuses it.

## Durable rule

A notebook represents a stable change in what the evidence supports, not the closure of a GitHub issue. Infrastructure work can enable research without becoming a research milestone.
