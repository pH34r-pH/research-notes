# Evidence model

Research Notes keeps mathematical, empirical, and interpretive strength separate.

## Formal checkpoints

A theorem can settle an exact mathematical claim under stated assumptions. It does not automatically establish that a trained network satisfies those assumptions or that the theorem explains an empirical result.

## Observations

An observation is tied to an experiment protocol, data, metric, and evaluation boundary. A measured endpoint does not by itself establish mechanism.

## Assumptions

Assumptions are surfaced rather than smuggled in as facts. An assumption may later become a theorem, an empirical test, or a reason to discard an argument.

## Hypotheses

Hypotheses organize tests; they are not promoted by repetition. When a control weakens a hypothesis, the public record keeps the earlier interpretation and the correction.

## Negative results

A negative result is bounded by what the experiment could have detected. “This intervention did not improve metric X under protocol Y” is stronger and more useful than “the mechanism does not matter.”

## Claim ladder

A useful reading discipline is:

```text
formal possibility / impossibility
        !=
measured accessibility
        !=
native utilization
        !=
causal importance
        !=
general mechanism
```

The project intentionally resists skipping steps in that ladder.
